How this review works

What you are checking

Each case is a real security fix from GitHub (a CVE), plus six files that a research benchmark uses as test material. You check that the facts and labels are right: the files labelled vulnerable really are vulnerable, and the files labelled safe really are safe. You are not judging any AI model, and you never see model results.

The screen

  • Left: your case list. Start at the top of Batch 1. A dot shows your progress: empty = not started, amber = draft, green = final.
  • Middle: the code. One tab per file. Compare with shows exactly what changed against the original.
  • Right: your review record. The blue box at the top always tells you the next step. Everything saves as you type.
  • Guide me (top bar) walks you through the current case one small step at a time: it highlights where to look and says exactly what to do. Use it for your first few cases.

Three rules

  1. Your bug location comes first. Write where you think the bug is and lock it. Only then does the case's official location appear.
  2. Every answer needs evidence: what you did and what you saw. Running code is welcome but not required. If you only read the code, tick Read / reasoned.
  3. Work independently. Don't look at any model output, and don't discuss cases with other reviewers until you have finished.

When you finish

Use Download my records any time to keep a copy. The study coordinator can also collect your final records from the server.
